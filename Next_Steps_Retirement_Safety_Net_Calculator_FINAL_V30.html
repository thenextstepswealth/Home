Fidelity: Fidelity's published guideline is to plan for savings to replace at least 45% of pre-retirement income after Social Security at a retirement/claiming age of 67. Its methodology shows 50% at age 65 and 40% at age 70 because Social Security and the retirement period change. Fidelity retirement-income guidance

Vanguard: Vanguard says a typical person may need about 70% of working income in retirement and estimates roughly 40% of that amount from Social Security, leaving about 30% to be funded from savings and other sources. This is an illustrative example, not a universal target. Vanguard retirement guidance

T. Rowe Price: Its published analysis uses a roughly 75% total-income replacement starting point. In one $100,000 household example, about 39% comes from Social Security and about 35% from other sources, illustrating the portion that may need to come from savings or other income. T. Rowe Price retirement-income guidance

These figures are published examples or guidelines from the organizations named above. They use different assumptions and are not individualized recommendations. The Next Steps calculator uses the user's selected retirement-income target and separately subtracts estimated Social Security.

Important: The percentage you choose here represents the portion of your pre-retirement income you expect to need from retirement savings and other non-Social-Security sources. Social Security is estimated separately and is not included in this percentage.

This is a retirement safety net—not a final retirement number. Reaching this target does not mean you should stop saving for retirement. It is a planning checkpoint to help you evaluate whether your Next Dollar may have another important job, including building your Golden Goose. Continue to review and adjust your plan as your circumstances and goals change.
Retirement safety-net calculation: The calculator estimates the amount needed at retirement by calculating the present value of the projected retirement-income shortfall over the planning horizon, using the selected return during retirement and inflation assumptions.

Planning horizon: The calculator uses age 102 as the fixed planning age. Retirement-income payments are calculated from the retirement age through age 102.

Important: This tool is for education and planning discussion. It is not investment, tax, legal or individualized financial advice.
Calculation note: Pre-retirement savings are projected annually using the selected return and contribution assumptions. Contributions are made through the year before retirement; the retirement year itself does not include a contribution. Retirement income is projected annually for the fixed age-102 planning horizon, with inflation applied to both retirement income and estimated Social Security. Social Security is shown as an editable planning estimate. Users can replace the auto-populated estimate with the annual amount shown in their SSA account.
