How this model works — and what it does not claim

  • Today's conversion tax is the increase in estimated 2026 federal income tax with the conversion versus without it. It includes the modeled effect of additional taxable Social Security and long-term capital gains stacking.
  • Future RMDs are estimated using the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table factors from the selected RMD starting age. The calculator isolates the slider-selected amount as a proportional slice of the traditional IRA.
  • Future RMD taxes use the future marginal tax rate entered above. The model assumes net RMD distributions are reinvested at the same annual return, and estimates tax on the remaining traditional IRA balance at the horizon as if it were then withdrawn.
  • The after-tax wealth comparison values the Roth balance tax-free and the traditional IRA balance after the assumed future tax. It also accounts for the opportunity cost of the conversion tax paid today by treating that amount as money that could otherwise have grown at the entered return.
  • For the comparison, tax paid from outside the IRA and tax withheld from the conversion have the same economic cost if both would otherwise earn the same return. The choice changes the Roth deposit amount, not the modeled overall wealth advantage.
